## PART 1: UNDERSTANDING THE PROBLEM STATEMENT (SIH26003)

### What the government is actually asking for

Strip away the corporate language and MDoNER is asking for one thing: **a digital cognitive-care companion for elderly people in NER, built for the region's actual conditions** — not a generic brain-training app translated into Assamese.

There are really three overlapping problems bundled into one PS:

**Problem A — Clinical/cognitive gap:** Elderly people in NER with dementia/MCI have no affordable, localized way to keep their brain engaged. Neurologists and geriatricians are scarce in hill and border districts, so early intervention (which is where cognitive training actually has evidence behind it) basically doesn't happen.

**Problem B — Caregiver burden gap:** Families are dealing with memory decline, confusion, anxiety, wandering risk, and social isolation in their elderly relatives, often while the younger generation has migrated out for work. There's no tool that lets a caregiver check in remotely or get early warning signs.

**Problem C — Infrastructure/access gap:** Even if a good app existed, NER has patchy connectivity, huge linguistic diversity (200+ languages/dialects), low digital literacy among the elderly, and almost zero culturally relevant content (existing apps use Western/generic imagery that means nothing to someone whose reference points are Bihu, Hornbill Festival, or their village landmarks).

The PS explicitly lists 8 required capability areas (a–h) — memory/attention/routine-recall/pattern-recognition games, AI-adaptive difficulty, multilingual voice interaction, culturally localized content, reminders (medicine/hydration/activities/appointments), caregiver dashboards, offline functionality, and elderly-friendly UI. That's your MVP checklist essentially handed to you.

### Why this problem is real (the evidence, so you can cite it in your pitch)

- **National scale:** LASI-DAD (Alzheimer's & Dementia, 2023) puts dementia prevalence in Indians 60+ at 7.4% — roughly 8.8 million people, projected to nearly double to 16.9 million by 2036.
- **Regional data gap:** Of the entire North-East, only Assam was even included in that study's detailed assessment. Sikkim has no state-level estimate at all. Nagaland, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh — essentially undocumented. This absence of data is itself part of your justification: nobody has built for this region because nobody has studied this region.
- **Risk gradients that hit NER hard:** prevalence is higher in women (~2x men), rural populations, and low/no formal education — all of which describe large parts of rural NER.
- **What the clinical literature actually supports** (important — don't overclaim): cognitive training has decent evidence for *prevention/early intervention* (ACTIVE trial) and *modest benefit in mild dementia* (Constant Therapy RCT, ~80% adherence, gains in memory/attention/arithmetic). It does **not** have solid evidence for reversing or curing dementia, and studies couldn't confirm it prevents progression from MCI to full dementia. So your honest positioning is: engagement + quality of life + early intervention — never "we detect/cure dementia."

### Current competitors and similar apps — full landscape

**Global / clinically-validated players:**
| Product | What it does | Why it doesn't solve this PS |
|---|---|---|
| **Constant Therapy (Brain+)** | Most clinically validated app in this space; targets dementia, aphasia, stroke, TBI recovery | English-centric, ~$300/yr subscription, no NER language or cultural content, no offline-first design for rural connectivity |
| **BrainHQ (Posit Science)** | Speed-of-processing training, descended from the ACTIVE trial lineage | Consumer brain-training, not dementia/caregiver-focused, no localization |
| **Lumosity, CogniFit** | Mass-market brain games | Entertainment-first, weak clinical backing, zero India/NER relevance |
| **Research-grade tablet/TV systems (nursing homes)** | Multi-domain cognitive training used in institutional care | Not consumer/home deployable, not built for rural low-connectivity settings |

**India-specific (your real reference points):**
| Product | What it does | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| **DemClinic** (Nightingales Medical Trust + ARDSI Bangalore) | Telemedicine-based cognitive screening/diagnosis platform | Clinical/diagnostic focus, not a daily-engagement gaming app, no NER language support |
| **DemLink App** (NMT) | Caregiver education + support access | Informational, not interactive/gaming, no adaptive AI |
| **Moving Pictures India** (NIMHANS) | Digital caregiver-education media | Available in English/Hindi/Kannada only — again, NER completely unaddressed |

**The gap, stated plainly:** No existing platform combines (a) NER-language localization, (b) NER cultural reminiscence content (Bihu, Hornbill, Chapchar Kut, local food/landmarks/music), and (c) offline-first architecture for low-connectivity hill/border districts. Every serious competitor is either clinically-validated-but-Western, or India-focused-but-Hindi/Kannada-only. That intersection is your entire competitive moat — it's not one clever feature, it's a combination nobody else has bothered to build because it's expensive and unglamorous (localization at scale).

### The honest challenges baked into this problem (acknowledge these in your pitch — judges will respect it more than a claim of a "dementia-curing AI")

1. **Localization at scale** is the core cost driver — 200+ languages means you cannot do all of NER at once. You *must* scope down (start with Assamese, expand later) and say so explicitly.
2. **Low digital literacy** among the elderly means voice-first and caregiver-mediated onboarding aren't optional extras — they're core.
3. **Connectivity** in hill/border districts makes offline-first non-negotiable, not a "nice to have."
4. **The "narrow games" trap** — apps that are just a grid of mini-games lose engagement fast. Literature says a holistic design (games + reminiscence + reminders + social/caregiver loop) retains users much better.
5. **Evidence honesty** — you must never claim diagnosis or cure. Frame everything as engagement, early intervention, and caregiver support.
6. **Validation** — a local partner (ARDSI Guwahati, a regional medical college) gives you legitimacy that a purely tech pitch won't have.

## PART 2: YOUR SOLUTION — FULL DESCRIPTION

### The one-line pitch

*"A localized, offline-first cognitive engagement and memory-assistance web/mobile app for elderly users in North-East India — built around games, reminders, culturally familiar reminiscence, and caregiver monitoring, with adaptive AI personalization and a roadmap toward NER-language voice interaction."*

Not a diagnostic tool. Not a cure. A daily-use companion that happens to generate the data that could power real clinical research later.

### Two user types, two interfaces

**1. Elderly user → the app itself (this is what you build as a web app first)**
- Plays cognitive games
- Sees reminders and reality-orientation info (day, date, "you are at home", next task)
- Browses/interacts with reminiscence content (family photos, cultural media)
- Eventually: talks to it in their own language

**2. Caregiver → a dashboard (can be a separate view/route in the same web app)**
- Monitors sessions, accuracy trends, reaction time trends
- Sets up reminders
- Uploads family photos and cultural content
- Gets alerts on missed reminders or meaningful performance changes

*(Clinician portal is explicitly a later extension, not MVP — don't let scope creep in here.)*

### Core feature set — organized by what's mandatory (from the PS) vs what's differentiating (your edge)

**A. Cognitive Games (mandatory, PS point a)**
Four solid games beats twelve mediocre ones for an MVP:
1. **Memory matching** — match pairs of cards, ideally with family/cultural photos instead of generic icons → exercises memory
2. **Attention/reaction game** — tap the correct target quickly → exercises attention + processing speed
3. **Sequencing game** — arrange daily-routine steps in the right order (e.g., "steps to make tea," "steps to get ready in the morning") → exercises executive function *and* directly ties into the PS's "daily routine recall" requirement
4. **Picture naming** — name an object/person from an image → exercises language and recall, and doubles as a way to weave in reminiscence content (naming a family member's photo)

You can later add: simple arithmetic, path/maze tasks (visuospatial), pattern/object recognition (explicitly called out in the PS description too).

**B. AI-Adaptive Difficulty (mandatory, PS point b)**
Don't overbuild this for v1. A rule-based staircase (3 good rounds → level up, 2 poor rounds → level down, else stay) is honest, explainable, and works from day one with zero training data. This satisfies the "AI/ML adapts difficulty" requirement without you needing a dataset you don't have yet — and you can honestly say "v1 uses rule-based adaptation; v2 introduces reinforcement learning/contextual bandits once we have real usage telemetry." That's a mature, defensible answer if judges probe your "AI" claims.

**C. Multilingual + Voice (mandatory, PS point c)**
Realistically for a hackathon-timeline MVP: pick **one** NER language (Assamese is the most defensible choice — it's the only NER state actually studied in LASI-DAD, meaning you can cite real prevalence data for it) + English. Voice can be scoped as "planned/prototype" rather than fully built — use an existing ASR/TTS API (AI4Bharat/Bhashini) for a basic demo rather than building your own model. Be upfront that full NER voice support is a post-MVP roadmap item requiring its own small speech-data collection effort (with proper consent/ethics, since your subjects are cognitively vulnerable).

**D. Culturally Familiar Content (mandatory, PS point d) — this is your strongest differentiator**
- Cultural reminiscence library: Bihu, Hornbill Festival, regional food, traditional clothing, local landmarks, regional songs
- Personal reminiscence: caregiver-uploaded family photos with metadata (year, people, location) → app prompts "Do you remember who this is?" / "Where was this taken?"
- This single feature does double duty: it's emotionally meaningful (reduces isolation, triggers positive recall) *and* it's a cognitive exercise (memory + language). No competitor does this for NER at all.

**E. Reminders (mandatory, PS point e)**
Medicine, hydration, meals, appointments — simple caregiver-set schedule, shown with large-text notifications. Pair this with a **reality orientation screen** (today's date, day, time, location, "next reminder") — this is a well-evidenced, cheap-to-build feature (Reality Orientation Therapy has real backing) that elderly users benefit from immediately.

**F. Caregiver Dashboard (mandatory, PS point f)**
- Sessions this week, average accuracy, average reaction time, games completed, missed reminders
- Trend graphs (accuracy over time, reaction time over time)
- Ability to create reminders, upload photos/content, manage reminiscence library
- Alerts for missed reminders or notable performance shifts (stated conservatively — "reaction time increased 18% vs personal baseline," never a diagnosis or percentage risk score)

**G. Offline-first (mandatory, PS point g)**
Critical because this is what actually distinguishes you technically. Local storage caches games, reminders, and recent content; results sync to the server when connectivity returns. Even as a web app, you can implement this with a service worker / local storage layer (PWA-style) so it degrades gracefully — this is worth demoing explicitly in your pitch since judges will specifically probe "what happens with no internet."

**H. Elderly-friendly UI (mandatory, PS point h)**
Large buttons, high contrast, minimal text per screen, simple navigation (no deep menus), consistent icon language, big touch targets, no jargon. This should visibly shape every screen you design — it's cheap to do right and painfully obvious when done wrong.

### What makes your version different from "just another brain-training app"

1. **NER-specific cultural + linguistic localization** — nobody else has this at all, let alone done well
2. **Offline-first as a real architectural choice**, not a checkbox — most competitor apps assume constant connectivity
3. **Holistic design** — games + reminders + reminiscence + caregiver loop, not a bare game grid (directly addresses the literature's "narrow games trap" finding on why brain-training apps lose users)
4. **Longitudinal data as a byproduct, not the product** — every session quietly builds a behavioral dataset (accuracy, reaction time, errors over weeks/months) that becomes the foundation for real personalization and, eventually, legitimate research on cognitive-decline markers — but you're not pretending you have that AI today
5. **Honest, staged AI roadmap** — rule-based v1 → telemetry-informed personalization v2 → RL/bandits v3 → decline-detection ML only after clinical validation. This kind of staged honesty is rare in hackathon pitches and tends to land very well with judges who've seen a hundred teams claim "AI detects dementia" with zero evidence.
6. **Caregiver-centered design**, acknowledging NER's specific demographic reality of out-migration — the caregiver may not be physically present, so remote monitoring matters more here than in a typical urban context.

### What you should explicitly say is NOT in scope (this matters for credibility)

- No dementia diagnosis, no "risk percentage," no MMSE/MoCA implementation in v1
- No full RL system, no custom-trained speech model, no speech biomarker research
- No full clinician/doctor portal
- No attempt at all 200+ NER languages — one language (Assamese) for MVP, clear expansion path after

### End-to-end flow (how it actually feels to use)

1. Elderly user opens app → greeted with today's date, a warm message, "you are at home," next reminder shown
2. Offered a short game (15–30 min sessions, matching real adherence data from trials) — say, memory matching using family photos
3. Plays it; system logs accuracy, reaction time, errors, difficulty; result stored locally if offline
4. Difficulty auto-adjusts next round based on the staircase rule
5. Later, caregiver logs into dashboard remotely, sees this week's sessions, accuracy trend, any missed reminders
6. Caregiver uploads a new family photo with names/relationship — it becomes tomorrow's "do you remember" prompt
7. Over weeks/months, this builds a personal baseline — which is what eventually lets you say (carefully) "reaction time is trending up relative to this person's own baseline" rather than any diagnostic claim
